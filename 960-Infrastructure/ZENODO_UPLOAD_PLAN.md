### Zenodo Metadata (copy-paste ready)

**Resource type:** Preprint  
**Title:**  
```
Policy Uncertainty and Gold Returns: Quantile Evidence of State-Dependent Safe-Haven Repricing
```

**Authors:**  
```
Özdemir, Mehmet Gökhan
Affiliation: Kırıkkale University, Department of Economics, Kırıkkale, Türkiye
ORCID: 0000-0002-6756-7285
```

**Description (abstract):**  
```
This paper provides the first quantile regression analysis of gold returns documenting a 
structural sign reversal in the EPU–gold relationship across the return distribution, 
certified with B = 999 bootstrap confidence intervals. In the lower tail (τ = 0.05), 
EPU carries a significant negative coefficient (β = −2.11×10⁻⁶, p < 0.001); in the upper 
tail (τ = 0.90), the coefficient is significantly positive (β = +1.84×10⁻⁶, p < 0.001). 
The OLS coefficient is statistically insignificant, explaining why earlier mean-based studies 
found mixed results. The federal funds rate exhibits a perfect monotonic reversal consistent 
with carry-trade and real-yield channel arguments. A Bai–Perron structural break (supF = 255.49, 
p < 0.001) in August 2001 is identified and confirmed via R strucchange. Results resolve a 
long-standing empirical puzzle: the safe-haven amplification and distress-state deleveraging 
channels coexist and cancel in the mean.
```

**Keywords:**  
```
gold returns, policy uncertainty, quantile regression, safe-haven, structural break, Bai-Perron, EPU
```

```

```

## PAPER 2 — Food Regime Decoupling
**File to upload:** `04-Manuscript/Makale2_SSCI_v02.docx`

### Zenodo Metadata

**Resource type:** Preprint  
**Title:**  
```
The Illusion of Decoupling in Global Food Regimes: Dietary Transitions, Structural Lock-In, 
and Carbon Leakage Across 138 Countries (2010–2022)
```

**Authors:**  
```
Özdemir, Mehmet Gökhan
Affiliation: Kırıkkale University, Department of Economics, Kırıkkale, Türkiye
ORCID: 0000-0002-6756-7285
```

**Description:**  
```
Using a balanced panel of 138 countries over 2010–2022 (FAOSTAT, World Bank WDI, BACI HS92), 
we estimate agricultural emission intensity using Driscoll-Kraay robust panel estimators. 
Key findings: (1) Agricultural emission intensity follows a U-shaped path with a structural 
turning point at approximately USD 1,907 GDP per capita, rejecting the Environmental Kuznets 
Curve for food systems. (2) Dietary diversity (Shannon entropy) carries significant positive 
coefficients in upper-middle and high-income countries (β ≈ 1.10–1.20, p < 0.01), consistent 
with Bennett's Law. (3) Agricultural import share is emission-increasing exclusively in the 
high-income group (β = 0.069, p < 0.10), documenting carbon leakage as a "Structural Decoupling 
Paradox." Capital accumulation saturates at approximately USD 7,853 per capita. These results 
demonstrate that apparent decoupling in high-income countries conceals offshore carbon 
displacement via trade, not genuine cleaner production progress.
```

**Keywords:**  
```
food system emissions, Environmental Kuznets Curve, carbon leakage, dietary transition, 
Bennett's Law, agricultural decoupling, panel data, global food regime
```

**JEL:** Q56, O44, Q18, F18, Q54, O13, Q15

## PAPER 3 — ZRY: ECI–GDP–ESG Nexus Turkey

**Journal target:** Emerging Markets Finance and Trade (editorialmanager.com/MEMFT) — $0

### Zenodo Metadata

**Resource type:** Preprint  
**Title:**  
```
Nonlinear Dynamics in the Economic Complexity–Sustainability Nexus: Evaluating the 
ECI–GDP–ESG Interaction in Türkiye
```

**Authors:**  
```
Özdemir, Mehmet Gökhan
Affiliation: Kırıkkale University, Department of Economics, Kırıkkale, Türkiye
ORCID: 0000-0002-6756-7285
```

**Description:**  
```
Using Türkiye's annual data for 2000–2023 (T = 24), we apply the Toda–Yamamoto (1995) 
modified Wald test to the ECI–COI–ESG–GDP–FDI system with Politis–Romano stationary 
bootstrap correction (B = 499). Of 20 directional hypotheses tested under asymptotic inference, 
three survive at conventional levels. After bootstrap correction, only the ECI → ESG channel 
survives at the 10 percent level (p_boot = 0.094). Asymptotic Wald statistics substantially 
overstate significance in N = 24 samples — a finding with methodological implications for the 
broader literature. We introduce the Ecological Productivity Paradox to organise the ECI–ESG 
relationship: structural complexity advances may trigger efficiency gains that activate a 
Rebound Effect, explaining why the complexity–sustainability nexus is not unconditionally positive. 
Results support a complexity-centred interpretation of Türkiye's net-zero 2053 strategy.
```

**Keywords:**  
```
economic complexity, sustainability, ESG, Toda-Yamamoto causality, bootstrap correction, 
Rebound Effect, Türkiye, emerging markets
```

**JEL:** Q56, O14, F63, C32, Q55

## PAPER 4 — AI Strategy Carbon DiD

**Journal target:** Energy Research & Social Science (editorialmanager.com/erss) — $0

### Zenodo Metadata

**Resource type:** Preprint  
**Title:**  
```
AI Strategy Adoption and Carbon Emissions: A Staggered Difference-in-Differences Analysis
```

**Authors:**  
```
Özdemir, Mehmet Gökhan
Affiliation: Kırıkkale University, Department of Economics, Kırıkkale, Türkiye
ORCID: 0000-0002-6756-7285
```

**Description:**  
```
This paper analyses 125 countries over 2005–2023 (40 treated, staggered adoption 2017–2021) 
to estimate the causal effect of national AI strategy adoption on CO₂ emissions. Using the 
Callaway–Sant'Anna (2021) heterogeneity-robust estimator and Goodman-Bacon decomposition, 
ATT = −0.055* (95% CI: [−0.095, −0.016]) — AI strategy adoption reduces per-capita CO₂ 
emissions by approximately 5.3 percent on average, building to approximately 9 percent by 
years 3–4 post-adoption. Event-study coefficients at k = −5 to k = −1 are all 
indistinguishable from zero (joint χ²(5) p = 0.962). Under HonestDiD (Rambachan & Roth, 2023), 
the negative effect is robust for pre-trend violations up to M̄ = 0.25. The standard TWFE 
estimator overstates the effect 4.5× due to the 2020 COVID-19 demand shock. Renewable energy 
share increases by 1.6 percentage points (ATT = +1.55, p < 0.01).
```

**Keywords:**  
```
AI strategy, carbon emissions, staggered DiD, Callaway-Sant'Anna, HonestDiD, Goodman-Bacon, 
energy transition, digital governance
```

## PAPER 5 — Labor Share Eurasian
**File to upload:** `04-Manuscript/main_v03b_JCE_20260426.docx`  
**Journal target:** Journal of Comparative Economics (editorialmanager.com/YJCEC) — $0

### Zenodo Metadata

**Resource type:** Preprint  
**Title:**  
```
Capital Deepening, Technological Progress, and the Labor Share: 
Panel Evidence from Eurasian Economies
```

**Authors:**  
```
Özdemir, Mehmet Gökhan
Affiliation: Kırıkkale University, Department of Economics, Kırıkkale, Türkiye
ORCID: 0000-0002-6756-7285
```

**Description:**  
```
Using the Common Correlated Effects Mean Group (CCEMG) estimator applied to 14 Eurasian 
economies over 2000–2022, we find a marginally positive panel mean-group estimate of the 
capital intensity coefficient (+0.114, SE = 0.058, p = 0.075) — inconsistent with the 
capital-displacing-labor narrative dominant in the OECD literature. Country-level heterogeneity 
is the central finding: Türkiye exhibits a precisely estimated negative effect (β = −0.194, 
p = 0.005), consistent with labor-replacing automation in manufacturing, while Georgia and 
Kazakhstan show positive associations consistent with capital-complementary technological change. 
Dumitrescu-Hurlin causality tests indicate bidirectional Granger causality between capital 
intensity and the labor share. This is the first application of CCEMG to the capital-labor 
share nexus in the Eurasian space, directly accommodating cross-section dependence and slope 
heterogeneity both confirmed in the data.
```

**Keywords:**  
```
labor share, capital deepening, CCEMG, Eurasian economies, cross-section dependence, 
panel heterogeneity, factor income distribution, transition economies
```

**JEL:** E25, J30, O33, P27, C23
