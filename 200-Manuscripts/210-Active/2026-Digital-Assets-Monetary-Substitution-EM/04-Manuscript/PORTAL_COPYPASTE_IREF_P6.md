## STEP 4 — Abstract (copy exactly)

```
This paper constructs a monthly panel for six large emerging market economies (Argentina, Brazil, India, Mexico, Nigeria, and South Africa) over 1973–2026-Q1 and tests whether digital-asset channels modify the transmission of macro fragility into exchange-rate depreciation. Three estimators designed for cross-section dependence and slope heterogeneity (CCEMG, AMG, MG-ARDL) converge on an inflation pass-through of +0.30 to +0.32, providing a robust multi-estimator benchmark. A Bonhomme-Manresa grouped fixed-effects decomposition reveals a two-cluster long-run equilibrium: Argentina and Nigeria exhibit near-unit elasticities (~1.0), while the remaining four economies show firmly anchored regimes (~0.005). The paper's main novel contribution is a proxy comparison: Google Trends search interest for Bitcoin yields uniformly insignificant coefficients across all specifications, whereas the Bitcoin local-currency price premium is significant under Webb wild cluster bootstrap (p = 0.045; 95% CI = [−0.0165, −0.0003]), consistent with a shadow exchange-rate channel. The GCAI triple-interaction documents Argentina's net dampening effect (β = −10.284***) as an institutional falsification test, while Türkiye's amplification (β = +9.573***) confirms that capital controls alone do not generate substitution. The findings imply that monetary substitution via digital assets is adoption-driven rather than attention-driven, with regime-specific implications for EM central banks navigating crypto-asset proliferation.
```
