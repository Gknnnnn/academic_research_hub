**"Macro Fragility, Digital Assets, and Monetary Substitution in Emerging Markets: A Quantile Panel Approach with a Proxy Evaluation"**

**Contribution.** This paper constructs a monthly panel for six large emerging market economies (Argentina, Brazil, India, Mexico, Nigeria, and South Africa, 1973–2026-03) and tests whether digital-asset channels modify the transmission of macro fragility — as proxied by inflation and broad-money instability — into exchange-rate depreciation. Three estimators designed for cross-section dependence and slope heterogeneity (CCEMG, AMG, MG-ARDL) converge on an inflation pass-through of +0.30 to +0.32, providing an unusually robust multi-estimator benchmark for small-N EM panels. More importantly, we document a two-cluster long-run structure: Argentina and Nigeria exhibit near-PPP elasticities (~1.0) while the remaining four economies show firmly anchored regimes (~0.005). This equilibrium split — independently corroborated by the Bonhomme-Manresa grouped fixed-effects estimator — implies that conventional aggregate EM estimates mask fundamentally different monetary regimes.

The paper's main novel finding is the *proxy comparison*: Google Trends search interest for Bitcoin yields uniformly insignificant coefficients across all specifications, whereas the Bitcoin local-currency price premium is significant under Webb wild cluster bootstrap (p = 0.045; 95% CI = [−0.0165, −0.0003]). This result distinguishes the adoption-based monetary substitution channel from attention-based speculation, directly informing current debates on the monetary implications of crypto-asset adoption in dollarised and fragile EM contexts. The GCAI triple-interaction further shows that Argentina's net dampening effect (−10.284***) serves as a natural institutional falsification test.

**Fit with IREF and gap addressed.** IREF regularly publishes empirical work on exchange-rate dynamics, monetary policy transmission, and digital finance in emerging markets. While the literature documents individual links between macro fragility and currency depreciation, no published study jointly tests: (i) convergence across CSD-robust panel estimators; (ii) a structured two-cluster equilibrium using grouped FE; and (iii) price-based versus attention-based crypto proxies under small-N inference correction. With only six country clusters, conventional cluster-robust standard errors are severely undersized; we apply the Webb (2023) six-point wild cluster bootstrap (B = 999) throughout, following MacKinnon and Webb (2017). This methodological contribution — mandatory for small-N EM panels — has not been applied in the existing IREF crypto-EM literature.
