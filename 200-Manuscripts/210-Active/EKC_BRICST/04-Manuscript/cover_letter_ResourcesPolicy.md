## Why this paper fits Resources Policy

*Resources Policy* has established itself as a leading venue for empirical research on the resource–environment–growth nexus in emerging and developing economies, including work on ecological footprint dynamics (Charfeddine & Mrabet 2017; Nathaniel et al. 2021), natural resource rents and environmental sustainability (Zafar et al. 2020), and the role of economic complexity in shaping resource use trajectories. The manuscript contributes to this line of inquiry in three ways directly relevant to the journal's scope:

**Natural resource rents as an explicit conditioning variable.** The augmented specification (M2) includes natural resource rents alongside economic complexity, renewable energy, urbanisation, trade openness, and FDI — placing resource dependence at the centre of the analytical framework. The CS-ARDL long-run estimate for resource rents conditions the EKC results, yielding findings directly actionable for resource-rich BRICS-T and MINT economies.

**Ecological footprint as a biocapacity-inclusive sustainability proxy.** Moving beyond CO₂ to the Global Footprint Network's ecological footprint per capita allows the paper to assess land, water, forest, and carbon demands simultaneously — a scope well-aligned with *Resources Policy*'s broad sustainability mandate.

**Second-generation panel methodology appropriate to the sample.** The paper deploys a full Pesaran (2004) CD → Pesaran–Yamagata (2008) → CIPS → Westerlund (2007) → CS-ARDL (Chudik & Pesaran 2015) workflow. Webb (2023) wild-cluster bootstrap (B = 999, N = 9) is applied throughout, ensuring inference is calibrated to the actual cluster size.

## Key findings

- **Renewable energy** is the structurally robust decarbonisation lever: the only variable that Granger-precedes ecological footprint reduction after correcting for cross-section dependence (bootstrap p = 0.038), supporting its prioritisation in national energy plans.
- **EKC inverted-U confirmed** for ecological footprint: CS-ARDL recovers β₁ > 0, β₂ < 0 in both M1 and M2; error-correction speeds (−0.83 to −0.99) are robustly negative across countries.
- **Economic complexity sign reversal** across the footprint distribution: β = −0.218 (p < 0.001) at τ = 0.10 rising to +0.075 (p = 0.001) at τ = 0.90 (Wald z = 9.41, p < 0.001); survives Russia exclusion (z = 6.52, p < 0.001). Complexity reduces pressure in low-footprint regimes but amplifies it in high-pressure regimes — invisible to mean-based estimators.
- **Honest inference:** no long-run elasticity is reported as 95%-significant given the N = 9 cluster constraint; causal claims are limited to the CSD-robust Granger result.
