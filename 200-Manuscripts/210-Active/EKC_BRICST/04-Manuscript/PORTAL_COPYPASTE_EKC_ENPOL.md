## TITLE (paste exact)

Economic Complexity, Renewable Energy, and the Environmental Kuznets Curve in BRICS-T and MINT Economies: A CS-ARDL Analysis of Ecological Footprint, 1995–2021

---

## ABSTRACT (paste into abstract field)

Does income growth eventually bend the curve of ecological pressure in large emerging economies, or does the inverted-U of the Environmental Kuznets Curve remain elusive once land, water, and carbon footprints are counted together? This paper revisits that question for nine BRICS-T and MINT economies over 1995–2021, using ecological footprint per capita — a biocapacity-relevant sustainability proxy that registers pressures well beyond carbon alone — as the outcome variable. The empirical strategy is fully second-generation: Pesaran–Yamagata slope-homogeneity and Pesaran CD diagnostics disqualify pooled first-generation estimators; CIPS unit-root tests and Westerlund (2007) ECM-based cointegration establish a stable long-run equilibrium; CS-ARDL delivers mean-group long-run elasticities; and Webb (2023) wild-cluster bootstrap (B=999) supplies honest inference under N=9 clusters. The bootstrap verdict is sobering but informative: no individual long-run elasticity is statistically distinguishable from zero, so CS-ARDL point estimates serve as directional signals rather than precise thresholds. Error-correction speeds (−0.83 to −0.99), however, are robustly negative — cointegration holds even when magnitudes cannot be pinned down. Pooled quantile regression (mixing between- and within-country variation; country fixed effects are not identifiable in the Machado–Silva sense at N=9 with T=27) corroborates the CS-ARDL inverted-U sign structure: GDP² is consistently negative at τ=0.25–0.90 (p<0.001), with implied turning points that are directional signals only (Webb bootstrap 95% CIs span zero in both specifications). The more striking result concerns economic complexity. The Economic Complexity Index reverses sign across the footprint distribution: β=−0.218 (p<0.001) at τ=0.10 rising to +0.075 (p=0.001) at τ=0.90 (Wald z=9.41, p<0.001), a pattern that survives Russia exclusion (z=6.52, p<0.001). Complexity dampens ecological pressure in clean country-years but amplifies it in high-pressure regimes — a non-linearity invisible to mean-based estimators. Renewable energy reduces the footprint robustly at every quantile, and remains the only predictor confirmed by CSD-robust panel Granger testing (Dumitrescu–Hurlin, 2012, with Webb bootstrap; boot p=0.038). Taken together, these results suggest that renewable-energy deployment and complexity-driven structural upgrading can support EKC-consistent trajectories, but only in ways that depend fundamentally on where an economy sits in the distribution of ecological pressure.
