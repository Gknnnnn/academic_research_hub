## TITLE

Financial Development and Economic Growth Nexus: Evidence from Factor Model-Based Panel Causality Approach

---

## ABSTRACT (construct from results)

This paper investigates the causal relationship between financial development and economic growth for G7 economies over the period 1980–2022. We apply the Nazlıoğlu and Karul (2024) factor model-based panel Granger causality test, which isolates idiosyncratic components via PANIC decomposition to address confirmed cross-sectional dependence. The PANICCA specification, preferred under small N, yields a strongly significant demand-following signal: economic growth Granger-causes financial development (Pm = 5.954, p < 0.001), while the reverse supply-leading direction is statistically weak (Pm = 0.494, p = 0.311). Country-level evidence from PANIC confirms demand-following for France, Italy, Japan, and the United States. Supplementary results from the Emirmahmutoğlu-Köse (2011) bootstrap causality test (panel stat = 45.693 > bootstrap CV = 22.871) and Dumitrescu-Hurlin (2012) test (bootstrap p < 0.001) corroborate the panel-level finding. The results align with Patrick's (1966) stage hypothesis: in mature G7 economies, real sector expansion precedes and generates the demand for financial services, rather than finance leading growth. These findings suggest that financial deepening policy should be sequenced to follow productivity-enhancing real sector investment rather than leading it.
