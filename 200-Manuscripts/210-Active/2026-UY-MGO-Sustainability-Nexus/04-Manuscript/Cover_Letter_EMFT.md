Dear Editor,

We submit our manuscript for consideration in *Emerging Markets Finance and Trade*.

This paper addresses an empirically contested question in emerging-market sustainability economics: does carbon accumulation constrain economic growth, and can migration-driven demographic pressures be channelled through green finance into decarbonisation outcomes? Using the Toda-Yamamoto (1995) modified Wald causality framework applied to Türkiye's annual time-series (2000–2023, T = 24), with Webb (2023) wild bootstrap correction — mandatory at small sample sizes — we find that **CO₂ Granger-causes GDP** (p_Webb = 0.040) as the sole robust channel, representing an environmental drag that survives size-correction. GDP → Green Finance is marginally supported at the 10% level. All migration channels collapse under bootstrap correction, documenting asymptotic size inflation in short-series studies.

**Three specific contributions justify submission to this venue:**

1. **Methodological rigour for emerging-market short series:** We document that asymptotic Wald p-values overstate significance by a factor exceeding five at T = 24. This result has direct methodological implications for the large Emerging Markets Finance and Trade literature that estimates causal channels on sub-30-observation national time series.

2. **A precisely identified environmental drag channel for Türkiye:** The CO₂ → GDP finding — surviving bootstrap correction — provides actionable evidence for Turkey's EU ETS alignment process and its 2053 net-zero commitment, both of direct policy relevance for EMFT readership.

3. **Transparent null results:** Three migration channels do not survive bootstrap correction. We report these explicitly, arguing that transparent null results are methodologically and substantively important for emerging-market sustainability research.

The manuscript is not currently under review at any other journal. All data are publicly available (World Bank WDI, EDGAR, OECD EPS). The replication code is available upon request.
