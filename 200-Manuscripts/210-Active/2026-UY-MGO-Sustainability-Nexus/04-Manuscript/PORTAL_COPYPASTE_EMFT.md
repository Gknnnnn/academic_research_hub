Nonlinear Dynamics in the Migration-Carbon-Growth Nexus: A Structural Evaluation of Green Finance and the Ecological Productivity Paradox in Türkiye

## Abstract (copy verbatim)
This study asks a specific causal question: does carbon accumulation Granger-cause economic growth in Türkiye, and is the migration-carbon nexus empirically identifiable using annual time-series data? Using annual data for Türkiye over 2000–2023 (T = 24), we apply the Toda-Yamamoto (1995) modified Wald causality procedure on a four-variable VAR system comprising labour migration (MIG), CO₂ emissions, green finance (GI), and GDP. All inference is based on Webb (2023) wild bootstrap with B = 999 replications to correct for severe size distortion of asymptotic χ² critical values at N = 24. CO₂ → GDP is the sole statistically robust causality channel (p_Webb = 0.040), consistent with an environmental drag on growth. GDP → GI is marginally supported at the 10% level (p_Webb = 0.100). MIG → CO₂ (p_Webb = 0.943) and MIG → GI (p_Webb = 0.194) are not robust — the latter appearing significant under asymptotic testing (p = 0.021) but collapsing under bootstrap correction, a textbook illustration of small-sample inflation. These results imply that Türkiye's path toward net-zero requires addressing the carbon-to-growth drag channel directly, rather than relying on migration-driven green-finance spillovers.
