## TITLE

Agricultural Productivity Falls Short of the Green Transition: Long-Run Panel Evidence from MENA and Sub-Saharan Africa

---

## ABSTRACT

Does improving agricultural total factor productivity (AgTFP) reduce energy consumption and carbon emissions in developing regions? Using a balanced panel of 25 MENA and Sub-Saharan African countries over 2000–2019, this paper tests whether AgTFP improvements lower agricultural energy intensity and, through that channel, CO2 emissions per capita. We apply the Augmented Mean Group (AMG) estimator to account for confirmed cross-sectional dependence (Pesaran CD = 10.60, p < 0.001) and heterogeneous long-run dynamics. The mediation channel does not materialise: AgTFP exerts no significant long-run effect on energy intensity (β = -0.040, p = 0.70) once income is controlled, and the bootstrapped indirect effect is indistinguishable from zero (95% CI [-0.119, +0.133]). Income per capita, not productivity, drives both energy efficiency (βGDP = -0.807, p < 0.001) and CO2 emissions (βGDP = +1.215, p < 0.001). A 1% increase in energy intensity raises CO2 per capita by 0.86% (p = 0.007), confirming that the energy efficiency channel matters – but AgTFP does not activate it in this sample. Agricultural modernisation alone is insufficient for a green transition in MENA and Sub-Saharan Africa; targeted energy pricing and technology policies are required alongside productivity growth.
