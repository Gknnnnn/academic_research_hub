## CHAPTER TITLE (paste exact)

Energy Dependency, Geoeconomic Vulnerability, and Power Asymmetries in the Eurasian Order

---

## ABSTRACT (229 words — paste into abstract field)

The reconfiguration of global power along multipolar lines has elevated energy security as a central determinant of macroeconomic resilience in the Eurasian order. Despite a growing literature on energy geopolitics and macroeconomic vulnerability, no panel econometric study has simultaneously examined how energy import concentration transmits into current account deterioration, exchange rate pressure, and constrained fiscal space across a coherent Eurasian panel conditioned on geopolitical alignment and institutional heterogeneity. This chapter addresses this triple geographic, methodological, and conceptual gap using panel data for 14 Eurasian economies over 2000–2022. Drawing on geoeconomic statecraft frameworks and cross-sectionally augmented estimators (CS-ARDL, CCEMG, AMG)—applied after Pesaran CD test results indicated statistically significant cross-section dependence (p = 0.040)—the analysis examines how asymmetric energy relationships translate into structural macroeconomic vulnerabilities. Empirical results consistently associate energy import dependency with lower current account balances: Pooled OLS yields β = −0.023 (p < 0.001); cross-sectionally robust CCEMG and AMG estimators yield β = −0.105 and β = −0.253, respectively (both p < 0.10); and the CS-ARDL error-correction coefficient (φ = −0.949, p < 0.001) is consistent with a persistent long-run adjustment process. The analysis further documents a sharp resource-rent asymmetry between net exporters (Azerbaijan, Russia, Kazakhstan) and net importers (Moldova, Belarus, Türkiye), which underpins the power asymmetries that structure SCO and EEU institutional frameworks. The chapter concludes with policy recommendations for energy diversification governance within EEU/SCO frameworks and bilateral energy partnership architecture.
