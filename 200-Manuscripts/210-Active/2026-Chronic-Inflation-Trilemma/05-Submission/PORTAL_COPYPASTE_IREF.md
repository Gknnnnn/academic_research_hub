## STEP 4 — Abstract (copy exactly)

```
This paper tests whether a country's position along the impossible trinity dimensions — monetary independence (MII), exchange rate stability (ERS), and capital account openness (KAOPEN) — determines the probability of sustaining chronic inflation, defined as CPI growth exceeding 10% for three or more consecutive years. Using a panel of 106 developing and emerging economies over 1985–2020 (3,474 country-year observations on the primary CPI variable; estimation sample N = 2,505–3,153 after trilemma merge), we find that exchange rate stability is the dominant anti-inflation device: a one-standard-deviation increase in ERS reduces the chronic-inflation probability by 19.8 percentage points (RE Probit, APE). Contrary to the conventional credibility-loss mechanism, monetary independence exerts a positive effect on chronic inflation (APE = +18.3 pp), consistent with the Barro-Gordon time-inconsistency argument: central banks retaining full monetary discretion — without the commitment device of an exchange rate anchor — exploit the inflation-output tradeoff, generating an equilibrium with persistently elevated prices. Capital account openness reduces chronic inflation through market discipline (APE = −7.5 pp). These results are robust to alternative thresholds (7%, 15%), sub-periods (1985–2000, 2001–2010, 2011–2020), and regional subsamples. The findings imply that exchange rate anchoring, rather than monetary autonomy, is the primary trilemma instrument for eliminating chronic inflation in developing economies.
```
