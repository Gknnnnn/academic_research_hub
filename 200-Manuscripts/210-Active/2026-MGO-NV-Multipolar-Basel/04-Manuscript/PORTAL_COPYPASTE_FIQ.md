## TITLE

Geopolitical Risk, Reserve Currency Fragmentation, and Banking Regulation: Evidence from COFER Data and the Russia Sanctions Shock

---

## ABSTRACT (construct from key results)

This paper examines how geopolitical risk drives reserve currency fragmentation and what the implications are for banking regulation under Basel III/IV frameworks. Using IMF COFER quarterly data (1999Q1–2025Q4), a trivariate VAR documents that geopolitical risk (Caldara-Iacoviello, 2022) Granger-causes changes in USD reserve share during the post-2016 RMB internationalisation era (F(2,96)=6.540, p=0.002), while the effect is statistically null in the full sample — consistent with regime-specific de-dollarisation dynamics. The Russia sanctions shock (2022) is evaluated via a staggered difference-in-differences design: Russia's Economic Complexity Index declined by approximately 77% relative to the never-treated control group (Brazil, China, India, Türkiye), with a HonestDiD confidence interval of [−0.071, −0.010] robust to parallel-trend deviations of up to 50% of the pre-treatment magnitude (Mbar=0.5). Ecological footprint increased by +26.0% post-sanctions. These findings are interpreted through the lens of Basel Pillar 2 capital adequacy: geopolitical shocks that fragment reserve holdings create unhedged sovereign risk concentrations not captured by current risk-weight frameworks. The paper contributes to the intersection of international monetary economics, geoeconomic statecraft, and macroprudential regulation.
